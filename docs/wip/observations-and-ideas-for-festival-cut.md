# Festival cut previsional plan
Length: 9–10 minutes. Not at 6. At six you'd have to cut either the murder or the meeting, and then it's a trailer, not a story. Plan for ten and treat six as out of reach.

## The irreducible chain

Nine things. If any one is missing, the short stops making sense.

1. A bomb aboard, timed to the contact moment — shown, not explained. The countdown display reading the arrival time does this in one shot.
2. Zao finds it; Harlan sees that she has.
3. Comms cut. One line that ordinary help can't arrive in time.
4. She sends a burst somewhere the audience can't identify. Overlay: 23 H 15 MIN.
5. Harlan at the door. "Who did you send that to?" One line of his reasoning. Murder.
6. The crossing — the one spectacle beat before the meeting, and it has to be juxtaposed with the murder or the throat-puts-the-ship-ahead idea has no image.
7. Time card. The burst arrives. Zao's own voice explains, once, why it could: the ship jumped ahead of its own warning and the light caught up. This is the title, and it's the only place the short can afford to state it.
8. Harlan exposed by her last words; wrist device; Elin rewinds the clock; 4-3-2-1 and the jump. "It isn't disarmed. It just isn't going off here."
9. Greeting, silence, the station answers, the sphere, the Velari lights the meeting. Then the last line.

## What goes, wholesale

The Prologue (replace with a ten-second cold open: protest feed, 43 MIN 18 S overlay — that's the whole premise the short needs). All of Sequence A except the dosimeter walk. The Sorell frame — B4b, C4–C9, all of D. The fuel-sabotage theory. The empty-space burst scene. The accomplice logic. Harlan's lament. E3–E5 — no objection, no authentication; her last sentence is the attribution. The chase — no shafts, no stairs, no third gravity dip as plot. Voss's apology. Voss's report.

That's most of the document, and it should be. The feature is about interpretation; the short is about a message outrunning its ship. Different film, same spine.

## What it costs, and what to protect

Cast: keep Zao, Harlan, Elin, Sorell speaking; Voss as a voice on the bridge; Okoye present but silent. Under twenty-five lines total.

Harlan is the casualty. With D gone he reads as a generic saboteur unless he gets one sentence of motive, and the only place for it is C2. Give him the door line — "We know what the mouth told us. That isn't the same as knowing who told it to speak" — and nothing more. It won't make him a character, but it makes him a position.

Elin can't be established as Zao's partner, so "Zao didn't miss" can't land; drop it. What can land is her receiving the burst and understanding, in one look, that it was aimed at them.

The withhold question resolves itself here: there is no time to explain the light-time trick twice, so the short *must* hide Zao's method in step 4 and pay it in step 7. That's the rev. 11 structure, and it's right for this cut regardless of what the feature does. The two versions can diverge on that choice; they share everything else.

One thing to keep that looks droppable: microgravity during the murder. Don't explain it, just play it — it's what makes the crossing that follows feel like the same physical event, and it's the only visual grammar the short has to say "this ship is doing something extraordinary while this is happening."
