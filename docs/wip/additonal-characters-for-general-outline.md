# Additional characters for general unconstrained outline

## This is for a future revision when we work on the longer scripts.

Issue: Would you add more characters to the full length feature film?
Quick response: Not aboard the ship. Possibly at Proxima, sparingly.

## Why the crew should stay at six

The plot is built on how few people there are. The frame works because Sorell is the only person who went aft; the blackout works because nobody can verify anyone's position; B2 works because six backs are turned; Harlan's consent argument is literally "six people on a research ship." A seventh crew member is a seventh alibi to account for, a seventh person who might glance at the shaft, a seventh suspect Elin has to clear, and a dilution of the one investigator the film has. Alien ran on seven; you have the right number for a ship this size and a story this tight. Every addition aboard costs causality.

The place you're actually thin isn't headcount, it's that one of the six is a job title. Okoye guards Sorell for a day and the outline never uses it. He's the only crew member who spends the far-side transit with her, hearing her insist on her innocence while he's the one who detained her, and he's the one who reads Harlan wrong in F1. That relationship — the guard who starts to believe the prisoner before the captain does — is free depth, costs no new casting, and gives D a second axis of doubt besides Elin's. Build him before you build anyone.

## Where a few small roles would earn their place

All at Proxima, all in the 36-hour window, none with more than a scene or two:

**A station director or mission authority.** Someone who makes the decision that the Ardor goes with the crew it has. This puts a human face on the thing Harlan objects to — that six unauthorized people were sent by one other unauthorized person under a deadline — and gives Voss someone he's answerable to, which he currently lacks. It's also the person who would eventually find the station-side transfer records, which lets G3's report land on Proxima rather than into nothing.

**A hazardous-stores technician.** One scene: Harlan, with legitimate authority, arranges for this person not to look at something. It shows his method — the true detail volunteered, the plausible reason, the calm — *before* the murder, so C6 and D5 read as a pattern rather than improvisation. It also seeds the records that will one day expose him. This is the most valuable add on the list because it fixes a real weakness: right now the audience never sees Harlan operate on anyone except Zao.

**Optionally, an Earth voice.** A mission controller whose messages arrive 43 minutes stale, always answering the previous question. It's the title dramatized at a third scale, and it can be a voice-only role in the Prologue and A1. The risk is that it's a device more than a character; use it once or twice or not at all.

## What I'd not do

Don't give Harlan a confidant. Someone who knows his views becomes a suspect and pulls the on-ship investigation off its clean axis. Don't add a medic to examine the body — the death is inflicted either way, and the examination adds procedure without information. Don't add anyone to the far side; the encounter's power is that the only human out there is one exhausted linguist on a tether.

Net: zero new crew, two small Proxima roles, one optional voice, and Okoye written up from inside. That's a feature with more people in it, not a bigger cast.
